How to Set Your Freelance Rate (Without Guessing)

By Gregory Patterson · August 2, 2026 · 5 min read

Most freelancers pick their rate the same way: they look at what they charged last time, add a nervous 10%, and hope the client doesn't flinch. That's not pricing — that's guessing with extra steps.

Here's a three-step method that takes the guesswork out.

1. Find your floor rate

Your floor is the number below which you are literally paying to work.

Add up your monthly costs: rent, food, software, insurance, taxes (set aside ~25–30% in the US), and a margin for savings. Say that's $4,500/month.

Now the critical part: you cannot bill 40 hours a week. Between admin, marketing, invoicing, and client-hunting, most freelancers bill 20–25 hours. Use 20 to be safe.

Floor rate = $4,500 ÷ (4.33 weeks × 20 billable hours) ≈ $52/hour

Anything below that number is a slow-motion emergency, no matter how good it feels to "win" the client.

2. Find your market band

Search job boards and freelance platforms for 10–15 listings in your specialty and note the rates. Ignore the top and bottom outliers. The cluster in the middle is your market band — for many writing and design niches it lands between $60 and $120/hour equivalent.

If your floor is below the band, price inside the band. If your floor is above the band, you have a business model problem, not a pricing problem — cut costs, specialize, or move upmarket.

3. Quote project prices, not hours

Hourly billing punishes you for getting faster. Convert to project pricing:

Project price = estimated hours × 1.4 × your target rate

The 1.4 multiplier covers scope creep, revisions, and communication overhead — the things every freelancer forgets to bill for.

Then offer three tiers:

TierWhat it isPurpose
EssentialThe bare deliverableAnchors low, catches budget clients
StandardDeliverable + revision round + faster turnaroundWhere most clients land
PremiumEverything + strategy call + priority schedulingMakes Standard look reasonable

Raise on a schedule, not on courage

Put rate increases in your calendar: every 3 months, or every 2 new clients, whichever comes first — up 10–15%. New clients get the new rate immediately. Existing clients get 30 days' notice. You will lose a client occasionally. You will still come out ahead.
